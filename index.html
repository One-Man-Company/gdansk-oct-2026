  • Saturday is your only reliable outdoor day. Dry from morning to night, cloud breaks after 19:00. Do the Old Town on foot, catch the 17:27 sunset on the water, then crawl the bars.
  • Sunday is a write-off outdoors. Rain from 14:00, 100% at 18:00, gusts to 62 km/h. Book the WWII Museum for 10:00, then go indoors: bowling, axe throwing or shooting, then a concert or a long dinner.
  • Monday is free-museum day. Every Muzeum Gdańska branch (Amber Museum, Town Hall) is free on Mondays. Do Oliwa's organ at 11:00, the free museums in the afternoon, sunset on a hill, then the airport.
  • Do not rent a car. Trams + SKM cover everything you need, and parking in the centre is 7.50 zł for the first hour. The one exception is Malbork — and that does not fit this trip.
  • Arrive by Uber XL, not two taxis. One 6-seat car is ~114 zł door-to-door versus ~200–240 zł for two official taxis.

Sunday — the storm day: museums, then indoors

Weather verdict: stay off the water and out of the wind. Monday alternatives — the "big swing" version
  • Malbork Castle (UNESCO, largest brick castle in the world): train 40–50 min, ticket ~70 zł, 4–5 hours on site. It would replace Oliwa and the free museums entirely, and you would need to be back by 18:30. Exhausting before a midnight flight, but unforgettable.
  • Gdynia: SKM 25 min, ORP Błyskawica + Dar Pomorza ~30–35 zł combined, then back. A good half-day if you have already seen Gdańsk's museums.
  • Rain contingency: if Monday turns wet, swap Oliwa for ECS (open Mon 10:00–17:00, 40 zł) and do the free museums — all indoors, all central.

What to actually pack (late October, Baltic)

  • Waterproof shell — not an umbrella. 62 km/h gusts turn umbrellas inside out.
  • Warm mid-layer + a hoodie for bars (Polish interiors are warm, streets are not).
  • Beanie and thin gloves — feels-like 1 °C on Sunday morning.
  • Waterproof shoes with grip — cobbles + rain = slick.
  • Power bank — maps + camera + tickets all day.
  • Passport/ID — required at the shooting range, and for tax-free.
  • One smart-casual shirt — for Sassy/Philharmonic, if you care.
  • Small backpack — no bags allowed in some museums; lockers are limited.

Weather analysis

Three days, three completely different problems: a dry cold Saturday, a gale on Sunday, and a mild dry Monday. Source: Open-Meteo hourly model blend, 0.25° grid, fetched 23 Oct 2026 07:35 EEST. Re-check the night before — 48-hour forecasts in October shift by 1–2 °C and a few hours of rain.

What the numbers actually mean

  • "Feels like" is the number to plan around. Saturday's 11.7 °C feels like 8.8 in the afternoon and 4.8 in the morning. Sunday's 9.6 °C feels like 5.2 — and on an exposed pier or the Westerplatte peninsula, add another 5 °C of wind chill. Dress for the "feels" column, not the headline.
  • Sunday's gusts are the real story. 62 km/h is not "breezy" — it is the threshold where ferris wheels stop, towers close, boats stay in port, and walking along the Motława stops being fun. The sustained wind is 22–24 km/h for six hours (10:00–16:00).
  • Sunday's rain has a schedule. 0% until 13:00, then 23% at 14:00, 46% at 15:00, 75% at 16:00, 100% at 18:00, easing to 64% by 21:00. So: do your outdoor move in the morning, be indoors by 14:00, and do not plan a walk home at 18:00.
  • Saturday's evening is the golden window. Cloud cover falls from 92% at 18:00 to 7% at 20:00 and 0% at 21:00. Sunset at 17:27, blue hour to ~17:55, then clear sky and city lights. This is when you want to be on the water.
  • Daylight is short. 9h56m on Saturday, shrinking to 9h47m by Monday. Effectively: usable daylight 08:00–17:00. Anything outdoors after 17:00 is a sunset/night activity, not a sightseeing one.
  • UV is negligible (0.95–1.8). No sunscreen needed; the risk is cold and wet, not sun.
  • No snow, no frost in the forecast — lows stay at 5–8 °C. The "Baltic in October" horror scenario is not happening this weekend.

Eat & drink

Gdańsk is cheap by Western European standards: a full meal in a milk bar is 25 zł, a good dinner is 60–80 zł, a cocktail 30 zł, a beer 12–15 zł, a vodka shot 8 zł. Tipping is 10% in sit-down restaurants, rounded up in bars.

The five meals you should not skip

  1. Pierogi at Mandu (28–45 zł) — the one dish everyone will ask about afterwards. Book.
  2. A milk-bar lunch at Neptun (~25 zł) — borscht, croquettes, plum pierogi. A cultural experience disguised as cheap food.
  3. Herring and vodka at Ministerstwo Śledzia i Wódki (45–60 zł) — five kinds of herring, ice-cold shots.
  4. Baltic fish at Ryba Piła (40–65 zł) — cod, herring, halibut, simply cooked.
  5. Brewery dinner at Brovarnia (70–90 zł) — pork knuckle, own beer, river views.

Getting there & getting around

Your base is at ul. Jacka Malczewskiego 89, Siedlce — a residential district 2.5 km south-west of the Old Town and 2.6 km from Gdańsk Główny. It is not a walk-to-everything location; it is a 10-minute tram ride or a 25–35 zł Uber.

Step-by-step: the train route (if you take it)
  1. Arrivals station: exit the terminal and follow signs to Gdańsk Port Lotniczy — a covered walkway, about 5 minutes.
  2. Ticket: buy in the Jakdojade or GoPay app, or from the machine. A single is 4.80 zł; a 75-minute transfer ticket is 6.00 zł and covers the tram leg too. A 24h pass is 18 zł and is the best deal if you will ride 3+ times.
  3. Train: take any SKM/PKM service towards Gdańsk Główny (some require a change at Gdańsk Wrzeszcz — check the board, it is the same platform). ~25 minutes.
  4. Tram: from Gdańsk Główny take tram 10 towards Siedlce/Emaus and get off at Zakopiańska (4 stops, 6 min), then walk 300 m. Or walk 25 minutes.
  5. Validate every ticket on boarding. Inspectors fine on the spot (~150 zł) and show no mercy to tourists.

3. Should you rent a car?

No — not for this trip. Everything you are doing is inside a 4 km radius served by trams, and the one thing a car helps with (Malbork, 45 min away) does not fit your departure day. A car in the centre means 7.50 zł for the first hour of parking, one-way streets, and a driver who cannot drink.

Money practicalities. Poland is card-first — Apple/Google Pay works in bars, trams (via app), museums and taxis. Carry 100–200 zł cash for small bars, tip jars and the odd milk bar. Currency is PLN (złoty), roughly 4.3 zł = €1. Do not use airport exchange desks; use an ATM (Santander/Euronet are near the flat) and decline "conversion to your currency". Tipping: 10% in restaurants, round up in bars, nothing in milk bars.

Local etiquette & safety

  • Gdańsk is very safe; the usual tram pickpocket advice applies around Główny at night.
  • No alcohol on the street in theory — in practice, drinking on the embankment or a park bench is normal and unenforced. Do not carry open bottles on trams.
  • Clubs: no flip-flops or sports shorts; Sassy enforces it, others do not.
  • Ask before photographing people; churches require quiet and covered shoulders.
  • Smoking is banned indoors in bars and restaurants — most have heated outdoor areas.

Three things that will actually go wrong (and the fix)

  1. You will be colder than you think. 9 °C with 40 km/h wind on a waterfront feels like 2 °C. Fix: waterproof shell + beanie, and plan bar-to-bar hops under 10 minutes.
  2. Sunday afternoon will be dead outdoors. Fix: book the museum slot and the indoor activity on Saturday night, not Sunday morning. Axe throwing and shooting need 24 h notice.
  3. You will cut it fine for the flight. A 23:59 departure means security by 22:15. Fix: leave the flat at 20:00, eat before 19:45, bags packed by 18:00 — and put the airport Uber on someone's phone with a 19:55 alarm.
